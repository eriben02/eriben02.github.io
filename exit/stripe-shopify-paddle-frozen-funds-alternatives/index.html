What happens when Stripe, Shopify, or Paddle freezes your payouts.

A 25% rolling reserve or 180-day hold can starve a healthy business overnight. And if a Merchant of Record drops you, you cannot take your subscribers’ card tokens with you. Here is how to protect your cashflow.

The short version: Standard payment processors (Stripe, Shopify Payments, PayPal) can impose a 25% rolling reserve for 90–180 days or a 120–180 day payout hold if your dispute rate crosses ~0.5%–0.75% or volume spikes unexpectedly. With Stripe, however, you own the customer relationship and can export PGP-encrypted card tokens. With a Merchant of Record (Paddle, 5% + $0.50), Paddle is the legal seller of record and owns the card vault; if Paddle terminates your account on a risk review, card tokens cannot be exported. To protect cashflow, pair Chargeflow (keeps dispute rate <0.5%) with a multi-processor failover rail like Whop Payments (2.7% + $0.30 base + 0.8% orchestration).

How to read this comparison: Payment processors do not have a single fixed-price “winner”. Your actual take rate and cashflow exposure depend on monthly volume, average order value, international card mix, and whether you require a Merchant of Record for global VAT/sales tax.

What contract terms & reserve docs say when an account is frozen

Why a Merchant of Record termination locks card tokens

With a Merchant of Record (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy), the MoR is the legal seller of record. Their entity appears on the customer’s bank statement, holds the tax registrations, and owns the payment vault, which means card tokens cannot be exported to your own Stripe account if the MoR terminates your agreement.

Source: Paddle Legal Terms & Merchant of Record Structure
Stripe rolling reserves & card-network dispute thresholds

Stripe’s underwriting engine can apply a rolling reserve (holding a percentage of each charge for 30–180 days) or a fixed reserve when dispute rates approach Visa/Mastercard monitoring thresholds (~0.5%–0.75%) or when delivery windows are extended.

Source: Stripe Documentation – How Rolling and Fixed Reserves Work

Common questions

How long can Stripe, PayPal, or Shopify Payments hold funds after an account freeze?

Stripe, PayPal, and Shopify Payments typically hold funds for 120 to 180 days following an account termination or permanent limitation to cover potential customer chargeback windows, or impose a 25% rolling reserve for 90 to 180 days.

Can you export subscriber credit card tokens if Paddle terminates your account?

No. Because Paddle operates as a Merchant of Record (MoR), Paddle is the legal seller of record and owns the card vault. If Paddle terminates your account following a risk review, you cannot re-tokenize or export subscriber cards, and every customer must manually re-enter their payment details on your new processor.

How do you prevent Stripe or Shopify Payments from placing a 25% rolling reserve?

Keep your dispute ratio strictly below 0.5% by installing an automated pre-dispute and chargeback prevention tool like Chargeflow, and maintain a secondary multi-processor orchestration checkout like Whop (2.7% + $0.30 base + 0.8% multi-processor orchestration) for immediate failover.
