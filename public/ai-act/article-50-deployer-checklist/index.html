Article 50 checklist for deployers

The answer first: If your organisation uses AI (you are a “deployer”) and it (a) runs emotion recognition or biometric categorisation on people, (b) publishes deepfakes, or (c) publishes AI-written text meant to inform the public on matters of public interest without human editorial review, you must tell people. You must do it clearly, at the latest at first exposure, and in an accessible way. This applies from 2 August 2026. AI Act Art. 50, Service Desk · AI Act Service Desk FAQ, when enforcement starts Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

Not legal advice. This page summarises official EU sources. Check the text of the law for your own case.

Who counts as a deployer?

A deployer is the organisation or person using an AI system under its authority. A natural person using AI for a purely personal, non-professional activity is outside the deployer duties. The Commission’s guidelines give examples (deepfakes of your own household, students’ homework, personal social media posts). Anyone gaining an economic benefit on a regular basis, or acting in a business, trade or freelance capacity, counts as professional. Commission Guidelines on Art. 50, C(2026) 5054 final Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

The provider of the tool still has its own duties (for example machine-readable marking under Art. 50(2)), even when the user is a private person. Commission Guidelines on Art. 50, C(2026) 5054 final Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

What is a deepfake here?

AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful. EU icons for labelling AI-generated content Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

What counts as text on matters of public interest?

The guidelines test three things: the text is published (open to a large, undefined audience, not a closed group or internal memo), it aims to inform the public, and it covers a matter of public interest (politics, public services, justice, public health, safety, environment, economic or scientific developments, etc.). Commission Guidelines on Art. 50, C(2026) 5054 final Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

Examples the Commission says are in scope: an AI summary of a town-council article on a newspaper site; AI-manipulated investor information in a listed company’s report; an AI-written storm warning on a weather institute’s social profile. Examples out of scope: AI-generated fantasy novels; ad copy and product descriptions (unless they make health, safety or sustainability claims); a chatbot news summary only the prompting user sees. Commission Guidelines on Art. 50, C(2026) 5054 final Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

What human review looks like in the Code of Practice

Signatories that rely on the editorial exception commit to a written policy that names the person with editorial responsibility (name, role, contact details) and describes the organisational measures and people who do the review before publication. They do not have to log each individual review. Media service providers already under editorial standards can use their existing procedures. Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

A note on emotion recognition

Emotion recognition in the workplace and in education is prohibited under Art. 5(1)(f) (subject to the narrow exceptions in that article), so the Art. 50(3) notice is not a way to make it legal there. Outside those settings, the Commission notes emotion recognition systems are also high-risk, so Art. 50(3) applies alongside the high-risk rules. Commission Guidelines on Art. 50, C(2026) 5054 final Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

Why there is a December date

The Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (published 24 July 2026), inserted Art. 111(4). Its recital 38 explains the four-month transition for providers already on the market. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, EUR-Lex · AI Act Art. 111, Service Desk Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

What happens if you get it wrong

Breaches of Article 50 can lead to fines of up to EUR 15 000 000 or 3% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Member States set the actual penalty rules and must consider the interests of SMEs. AI Act Art. 99, Service Desk Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
