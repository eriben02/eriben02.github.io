ETSI TS 104 275 explained

The answer first: ETSI TS 104 275 is a free technical specification from ETSI’s committee on Securing AI. It tells developers and operators of generative AI how to reduce the chance their systems produce harmful deepfakes: risk assessment before release, human oversight, labelling and filters in the UI, training, and documented evidence, with pass/fail test cases. It is not a harmonised standard under the AI Act and does not by itself prove Article 50 compliance. ETSI TS 104 275 V1.1.1 PDF · ETSI press release, 29 Oct 2026 · ETSI work programme, DTS/SAI-0031 Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

Why two dates?

The ETSI work programme lists the publication milestone and cover date as 29 Sep 2026, and the PDF metadata is dated the same day. ETSI’s press release “ETSI unveils new standard to reduce production of harmful deepfakes” is dated 29 Oct 2026. If you see “released 29 October”, that is the announcement; the document itself is dated September 2026. ETSI TS 104 275 V1.1.1 PDF · ETSI press release, 29 Oct 2026 · ETSI work programme, DTS/SAI-0031 Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

Scope in one sentence

A deepfake-focused application of EN 304 223 to generative AI, covering misinformation, disinformation, loss of confidentiality, copyright, IPR and LLM training and source material, with more prescriptive provisions where needed. ETSI TS 104 275 V1.1.1 PDF Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

Who it is for

Mainly developers and system operators of generative AI (some provisions also name data custodians). It is not written for people who only publish AI content, though its UI provision is relevant to designers. ETSI TS 104 275 V1.1.1 PDF Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

Annex C: test cases with pass/fail

Annex C (normative) gives a test purpose, method and pass/fail verdict per extension provision. Example: for the UI provision, a pass needs design and implementation evidence of content filters, clear AI-content labelling and user information on limits, and test results showing they work. For training, a pass needs GenAI security topics in training material reviewed within the last 12 months. ETSI TS 104 275 V1.1.1 PDF Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

How it relates to the EU AI Act
  • ETSI says the TS “supports objectives laid out in the EU AI Act, extending security beyond systems classified as high-risk.” ETSI press release, 29 Oct 2026 Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • The ETSI work programme marks it as not a harmonised standard. ETSI work programme, DTS/SAI-0031 Accessed 30 Oct 2026. So it does not give a presumption of conformity.
  • For Article 50 labelling and marking, the Commission’s recognised tool is the Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content, which the Commission and AI Board assessed as adequate. Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content · Commission news, strong backing for the Code of Practice Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • The TS mentions labelling only at a high level (“clearly labelling AI-generated content”); it does not specify icons, placement or C2PA. For those, see the Code and the EU icons. ETSI TS 104 275 V1.1.1 PDF · EU icons for labelling AI-generated content Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
Annexes A and B

Annex A (informative) gives an overview of detection and prevention of GenAI deepfakes, including reporting and removal. Annex B (informative) maps GenAI to trustworthy-AI concepts. ETSI TS 104 275 V1.1.1 PDF Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

What a designer can take from it

  1. Labelling is a design requirement, not a legal footnote (5.1.2-1).
  2. Keep evidence: design docs and test results showing the label and filters work (Annex C).
  3. Tell users what the system can’t do reliably (5.1.2-1).
