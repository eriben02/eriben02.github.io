C2PA and watermarking tools compared

Under the EU Code of Practice, providers of generative AI are expected to use two layers of machine-readable marking: digitally signed metadata (what C2PA / Content Credentials does) plus an imperceptible watermark (what SynthID or Digimarc do). Free-form text cannot carry metadata, so a watermark alone is considered sufficient there. Neither layer replaces the visible label a deployer must show. Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content · AI Act Art. 50, Service Desk Accessed 30 Oct 2026.

Which do I need? (provider vs deployer)
  • You build or sell a generative AI system (provider): Art. 50(2) applies to you. The Code expects signed metadata and a watermark for audio, image, video and containerised text. Systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026 have until 2 Dec 2026. AI Act Art. 50, Service Desk · Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content · AI Act Art. 111, Service Desk Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • You only use AI to make content (deployer): your legal duty is the visible disclosure under Art. 50(4). C2PA and watermarks are useful (they survive sharing and back up your label), but they are not the label. See labelling patterns. AI Act Art. 50, Service Desk Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
C2PA / Content Credentials, details
  • C2PA describes itself as “an open technical standard for publishers, creators and consumers to establish the origin and edits of digital content. It’s called Content Credentials.” C2PA Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • The spec index lists versions up to 2.5, plus a Soft Binding API (the hook that lets a watermark point back to a manifest). C2PA specifications, 2.5 Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • The “cr” pin is a C2PA trademark used by conforming products. CAI open-source SDK Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • The C2PA is a standards body under the Linux Foundation’s Joint Development Foundation; the CAI is an Adobe-led consortium that maintains the open-source SDK. CAI open-source SDK Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
Adobe Content Authenticity, details
  • Free web app at contentauthenticity.adobe.com, in public beta. Adobe Content Authenticity (Beta) · Adobe blog, public beta, 24 Apr 2025 Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • Upload up to 50 JPG/PNG files at a time, max 20 MB each; credentials cannot be edited after applying. Adobe Content Authenticity (Beta) Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • Adobe states credentials are stored in the file and in Adobe’s public Content Credentials cloud, linked through embedded metadata, invisible watermarking and digital fingerprinting. Adobe Content Authenticity (Beta) Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • Support for video and audio was announced as “coming soon” in April 2025; we did not find a confirmation of availability. Unknown. Adobe blog, public beta, 24 Apr 2025 Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
Google SynthID, details
  • Google says SynthID has watermarked over 180 billion images and videos and 240,000 years of audio, and that the SynthID Detector became available globally in English on 7 Oct 2026 (post updated 29 Oct 2026). Google blog, SynthID Detector Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • The detector checks images, video and audio made with AI from Google or partners named as OpenAI, NVIDIA, Kakao and Apple. Google blog, SynthID Detector Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • SynthID Text is open source; detection is probabilistic (watermarked / not watermarked / uncertain). Thorough rewriting or translation can greatly reduce detector confidence. Google SynthID Text Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • Pricing for the detector or for third-party image, audio or video watermarking: unknown.
Digimarc, details
  • Digimarc says it built “the first implementation of invisible watermarking for C2PA purposes” and that its watermark keeps a link to Content Credentials even if media is manipulated or stripped of metadata. Digimarc provenance and authenticity Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • It positions the product for the EU AI Act among other regulations. Digimarc provenance and authenticity Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • Pricing: unknown (request a demo). Digimarc provenance and authenticity Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
Truepic, details
  • Truepic’s current site focuses on authenticated photo and video inspections (insurance, lending, warranty). Truepic Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • Its Lens API docs describe signing captures with a key and certificate from Truepic’s Certificate Authority and returning a JSON report of the content credentials; sample output shows C2PA manifest data. Truepic Lens API Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • Public pricing exists for the inspection product (Flex: $1,000/month minimum; Standard shown at $1,450/month for a selected tier). Truepic pricing Accessed 30 Oct 2026. Truepic is a capture-provenance tool, not an AI-output labelling tool.

What none of these do

  • They do not decide whether your content is a “deepfake” or “public-interest text”. That is a judgment under Art. 50(4). AI Act Art. 50, Service Desk Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
  • Watermark detection is probabilistic; metadata can be stripped. That is why the Code asks for layers. Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content · Google SynthID Text Accessed 30 Oct 2026.
